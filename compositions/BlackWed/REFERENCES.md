# Black Wednesday — References

## Historical Context

### The Event
- **Date:** 16 September 1992 ("Black Wednesday")
- **Core event:** The UK was forced to withdraw the pound sterling from the European Exchange Rate Mechanism (ERM) after failing to keep the currency above its agreed lower limit.
- **Key figure:** George Soros's Quantum Fund short-sold approximately £10 billion worth of pounds, profiting an estimated £1 billion.
- **Exchange rate:** The pound fell from ~$2.00 to $1.5090 against the US dollar on that day.
- **Interest rates:** The Bank of England raised interest rates from 10% to 12%, then to 15% in a single day, before retreating.

### The Dealing Room
- 1992-era trading floors: CRT monitors (amber or green phosphor), physical telex machines, paper trading tickets, open-outcry alongside electronic systems.
- Aesthetic: dark carpet, mahogany or dark wood desks, institutional fluorescent lighting overhead, individual desk lamps (brass or adjustable arm), thick cables and wires.
- The mood: controlled panic. Professional calm masking genuine fear.

### Bank of England
- **Location:** Threadneedle Street, City of London.
- **Architecture:** Neoclassical, designed by Sir John Soane (1788), rebuilt by Sir Herbert Baker (1920s-30s). Portland limestone facade. Iconic columns — Ionic order, 6 on the main Threadneedle Street entrance.
- **Night appearance:** Severely imposing. Sodium street lighting casts warm orange on cold grey stone. The building is dramatically lit (or was) by institutional spotlights that highlight the columns.
- **Metaphor:** The building as monolith — immovable, permanent, indifferent to the chaos within. It stands even as its authority crumbles.

## Visual References

### Cinematic
1. **"Margin Call" (2011)** — The sterile, fluorescent-lit trading floor. Night shifts. The tension of people who know something terrible is happening while appearing calm. Cool color temperature with warm desk lamp pools.

2. **"The Big Short" (2015)** — Data as visual element. Numbers on screens becoming emotional actors. The fracture between what screens show and what they mean.

3. **"Inside Job" (2010)** — Documentary aesthetic. Institutional buildings at night. The gap between the grandeur of architecture and the recklessness inside.

4. **"Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy" (2011)** — 1970s British institutional interiors. Desaturated warm palette. Overhead fluorescents. The claustrophobic warmth of bureaucratic spaces.

5. **"The Iron Lady" (2011)** — Bank of England exterior shots. Threadneedle Street at night. The visual weight of institutional power.

### Photographic
1. **Martin Parr** — British institutional interiors. The unflattering honesty of fluorescent light on ordinary spaces.

2. **Gregory Crewdson** — Cinematic still photography. Single light sources illuminating portions of larger dark scenes. The feeling of narrative in a single image.

3. **Todd Hido** — Night exteriors. Sodium vapour street lighting. Houses lit from within. The warmth-from-outside effect.

### Design / Motion
1. **Film grain overlay** — Not digital noise. The organic quality of 16mm or Super 8 grain. Warm film stock look (Kodak 5219 / Vision3 500T).

2. **Terminal/telex typography** — Dot matrix or early CRT font character. Green-on-black phosphor. The aesthetic of 1992 financial computing.

3. **Chromatic aberration** — As emotional punctuation, not decoration. Used at the moment of "breaking" — the £ shattering.

4. **Screen shake** — Physical impact metaphor. The market crash as an earthquake. Decaying oscillation, never random — always derived from sine with damping.

## Color Reference Sources

### Paper Warm (#F5E6C8)
- Aged financial documents. Slightly yellowed bond paper. Not bright white (modern laser print) but the warm off-white of 1992 continuous-feed printer paper.

### Phosphor Green (#33FF66)
- P31 phosphor CRT monitors common in financial terminals. Not the amber P3 phosphor (Bloomberg) — the green of Telerate, Reuters terminals of the era. Higher saturation than real P31 but reads correctly on modern displays.

### Stone Grey (#8B8680)
- Portland limestone under sodium vapour light at night. Not the daytime warm cream but the night-shifted grey that happens when warm orange light hits warm cream stone.

### Void Text (#E8DCC8)
- Warm cream text on dark backgrounds. The color of light falling on old paper. Deliberately not pure white — white would be anachronistic and harsh.

### Shard Color (#C9A84C)
- Old gold. Not bright modern gold (#FFD700) but the darker, more complex gold of gilt picture frames, gold leaf on institutional buildings, sovereign coins.
